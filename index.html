Data cleaning

Cleaning three messy datasets from three sectors

Python (pandas, NumPy) · OpenRefine (GREL) · Excel

Agriculture, fintech and retail data, each with a different kind of mess. I explored each one, wrote repeatable cleaning scripts, and kept a log of every change so anyone can check the work or re-run it on new data.

Most of an analyst's time goes into getting data ready. This project shows I can find the problems, choose the right tool for each one and leave a record others can follow.

What was wrong

  • Duplicate submissions, some only visible once names were fixed
  • The same crop spelled many ways: Maiz Mahindi Maize (grain)
  • Numbers stored as text: 139kg KES 10896.15 "3,364"
  • Negative and missing quantities and prices
  • Nine date styles, plus dates that cannot exist: 2024-02-30 2025/13/01
  • Impossible GPS points (987.654) and latitude and longitude typed in each other's columns
  • Misspelled sub-counties: DagorettiNorth Kasarni Roy Samba

How I cleaned it
  1. Trimmed spaces, fixed casing and removed duplicates, then checked for duplicates again at the end, because some rows only match once their crop names are fixed.
  2. Mapped 53 spellings, including Swahili names such as mahindi, waru, mtama and ndengu, to 24 standard crop names.
  3. Pulled numbers out of text, cleared impossible negative values and filled gaps with the median for that crop.
  4. Converted all nine date styles to YYYY-MM-DD. Dates that cannot exist were left blank instead of guessed.
  5. Converted compass coordinates (1.32653S) to decimal degrees, swapped reversed pairs back and removed points outside Nairobi.
  6. Matched every district to Nairobi's constituencies and standardised phone numbers to one format.

What was wrong

  • Names typed inconsistently: Mohamed, James Kamau. Makori Collins-khamisi
  • Mobile numbers with an extra 0 after +254, or prefixes no Kenyan mobile uses
  • Dates mixing day-first and month-first in the same format: 21-05-2025 04-18-2025
  • An interest rate of exactly 5% far more often than any other, likely a filled-in default
  • Loan amounts in two very different size groups

I explored the data in OpenRefine first, because its facets show every variant of a value at once. Then I wrote the final rules in pandas so they can be re-run on next quarter's data.

How I cleaned it
  1. Turned every name into First Last: reversed Surname, First entries and removed stray full stops and hyphens.
  2. Wrote every number as +254 plus the local number. The 29 that fail the Kenyan mobile check were cleared rather than guessed.
  3. Used the fact that every application is from April to June 2025 to tell day-first from month-first dates. The 6 that could go either way follow the Kenyan day-first convention.
  4. Applied the KES 100 to 1,000,000 range rule (no loans fall outside it) and recorded that loans split into two groups, so they should be analysed separately.
  5. Recorded the suspicious 5% interest rate in the cleaning log rather than changing the values.

What was wrong

  • 15 transactions recorded twice
  • Seven date styles, with day-first and month-first both in use: 12-04-2025 01/29/2025 26.05.25
  • Currency text inside prices: KES 209.23 KSh 396 Sh. 214
  • Totals divided by 1,000: Sh. 214 × 1 = 0.21
  • One product under many names: Maziwa 1L SUGR unga ya mahindi
  • Blank or inconsistent categories (Grocery Groceries Food) and five ways of writing M-Pesa or card

How I cleaned it
  1. Removed the 15 duplicate transactions, so every Transaction ID is unique.
  2. Read each date style with its own rule, then confirmed the rules by checking that every date lands between January and June 2025.
  3. Merged 55 product spellings into 22 products and moved brand names (Colgate, OMO, Fanta) into their own column, so that information isn't lost.
  4. Rebuilt each category from the product, so a product always sits in one category.
  5. Removed currency text, then recalculated every total that didn't equal quantity × unit price.
  6. Unified payment methods into M-Pesa, Card and Cash. The 19 sales with no price stay blank, because an invented price would distort revenue.

Power BI · synthetic Safaricom-style customer data

Telecom companies lose revenue every time a subscriber leaves. I cleaned a customer dataset and built an interactive Power BI dashboard to show who churns and what they have in common, so a retention team knows where to focus.

Research

Economic growth and household debt in Kenya, 2010–2023

Time-series econometrics · Karatina University

As household borrowing grew alongside mobile money and digital credit, I designed a study to test whether Kenya's economic growth drives household debt, or the other way round. I set out the data plan and the econometric method.

Method, step by step

  1. Collect time series from CBK, KNBS, IMF and World Bank
  2. Unit root tests (ADF, Phillips–Perron)
  3. Johansen cointegration
  4. VECM or ARDL model
  5. Granger causality
  6. Diagnostic tests
Variables and data sources
  • Growth and prices: real GDP growth, inflation and private consumption (KNBS)
  • Debt and credit: household debt, private-sector credit, non-performing loans and lending rates (Central Bank of Kenya)
  • Financial inclusion: account access, mobile money and digital credit uptake (FinAccess surveys, FSD Kenya)
  • Cross-checks: IMF and World Bank series to verify national figures

Quarterly data gives up to 56 observations for 2010–2023, enough to separate short-run swings from long-run relationships.
